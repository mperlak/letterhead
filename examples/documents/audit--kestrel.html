Westerhaven tenant: access and integration audit before the missed-collection pilot

The Westerhaven tenant does not meet Annex C v2.1 today. One shared API key lets about 20 people at ReinNet rewrite tomorrow's routes. That has to be fixed before the meldingen system is connected. The other seven findings are small, and each one has an owner and a date before the pilot starts on Mon 11 January 2027.

Verdict

Does not meet Annex C v2.1 as of 14 October 2026. We found 8 findings: 1 critical, 4 major and 3 minor. Four Annex C requirements fail (C4, C7, C9, C11). Three integration-guide rules fail too. Two of those stop working in production on Mon 30 November.

None of the fixes is large. The pilot can go ahead if the shared API key (finding 1) is replaced before the tenant is connected to the meldingen system, and if the majors are closed by the dates in the remediation plan.

Scope and criteria

Ruth Okafor carried out the review on Tue 13 and Wed 14 October 2026. It was a review of configuration and logs, not a penetration test, and nothing was attacked. We looked at the ReinNet side read-only, with Kees Brouwer on screen share.

In scope

  • The Westerhaven tenant in production on Kestrel 4.3: 14 trucks and 31 planner and dispatcher user accounts.
  • The ReinNet fleet connector, which sends GPS and lift events.
  • ReinNet's dispatch dashboard and the webhook receiver behind it.
  • The meldingen system webhook. It exists only on staging so far.

Not in scope

  • The internals of Westerhaven's meldingen system, and the municipal app.
  • Operating-system hardening of the cab tablets. ReinNet's MDM manages these.
  • Physical security of the depot.
  • Other tenants on Kestrel.
  • The "return stop" feature for the pilot. It has not been built yet.

Criteria

We checked against Annex C of the Westerhaven contract, Information security requirements for suppliers, version 2.1 (requirements C1 to C12). We also checked the webhook and API key rules in the Kestrel integration guide 4.3. Those rules have no Annex C number.

Findings

Finding 1

One API key shared by three ReinNet systems, in plain text on SharePoint

criticalAnnex C7API key reinnet-shared

Observed. Three systems use the key reinnet-shared: the fleet connector, the ReinNet dashboard, and an Excel macro that Kees runs every morning to pull the plan. The key can read and write routes and stops. It was created on 14 February 2025 and has never been rotated. In the last 30 days it made 41,000 calls from 3 IP addresses. The key is stored in plain text in a shared sheet on ReinNet's SharePoint, which about 20 people can open. Kees showed us the sheet.

Why it matters. Anyone who can open that sheet can rewrite tomorrow's routes. C7 asks for one key per system, the least privilege each system needs, and rotation at least once a year. This key fails all three. The macro only reads the plan, so it does not need write access.

Fix. Issue three keys: one for the connector, one for the dashboard, and a read-only key for the macro. Switch each system to its new key, then delete reinnet-shared and remove it from the sheet. This takes about half a day on the ReinNet side. Kestrel creates the keys the same day they are requested.

Finding 2

Seven accounts still belong to people who have left

majorAnnex C4Tenant user list

Observed. 7 of the 31 user accounts belong to people who have left ReinNet or the municipality. Their last logins were between February 2025 and March 2026. Two of the seven have the planner admin role, which can edit routes and add users. Nobody has logged in with any of the seven since then, and none has a password reset pending.

Why it matters. C4 requires accounts to be removed within 5 working days of someone leaving. At the moment there is no process that tells Kestrel when someone leaves.

Fix. Kees confirms the list of seven in writing, and Kestrel removes the accounts the same day. After that, ReinNet HR emails Kestrel support whenever someone leaves.

Finding 3

No MFA on the planner admin role

majorAnnex C9Tenant security settings

Observed. None of the 5 planner admins uses a second factor. Kestrel supports TOTP, but the setting is off for this tenant.

Why it matters. C9 requires MFA for admin access. Planner admins can edit routes and manage users.

Fix. Kestrel turns on TOTP for the planner admin role, and the 5 admins enrol an authenticator app.

Finding 4

ReinNet's webhook receiver only checks the old SHA-1 signature

majorIntegration guide 4.3ReinNet dashboard, plan-published webhook receiver

Observed. The receiver for plan-published events only checks the old X-Kestrel-Signature header (SHA-1). Kestrel 4.3 added the Kestrel-Signature header (SHA-256). We stop sending the old header on Mon 30 November 2026.

Why it matters. From 30 November the receiver will reject every event, and the dashboard will stop getting published plans. The quick way out, turning the check off, would leave the dashboard accepting events from anyone.

Fix. Verify the Kestrel-Signature header (SHA-256) instead. Kees estimates a 5-line change. Bram at ReinNet makes it. Kestrel can send test events to ReinNet's staging on any day.

Finding 5

The fleet connector still calls the v1 API, which is removed on 30 November

majorIntegration guide 4.3ReinNet fleet connector, /v1/routes and /v1/stops

Observed. The connector still calls /v1/routes and /v1/stops. API v1 will be removed on Mon 30 November 2026. ReinNet has been told, but the move to v2 has not been scheduled yet.

Why it matters. This is about availability more than security. From 30 November, routes and stops will stop reaching the trucks through the connector. The fix belongs to the fleet vendor, not to the dashboard team that owns finding 4. That is why the two are separate findings.

Fix. Move the connector to /v2. Kees asks the fleet vendor to do it. If the vendor will not commit, Kees tells Kestrel by Fri 6 November and we agree a stopgap together.

Finding 6

Kestrel staging accepts unsigned webhooks from the meldingen system

minorIntegration guide 4.3Kestrel staging, "allow unsigned" flag

Observed. Westerhaven's meldingen system sends its webhook calls to Kestrel staging without an HMAC signature. Our staging accepted them because the "allow unsigned" flag is on there. This is the endpoint we will use for pilot testing. In production the flag is off; we checked.

Why it matters. Exposure is low because this is staging only. If the pilot is tested without signatures, though, the first signed call will happen in production.

Fix. Kestrel turns off "allow unsigned" on staging, and the meldingen supplier signs its staging calls. Ruth handles this with Joost, the integrator at the municipality's supplier.

Finding 7

Plan export links last 7 days and need no login

minorPlanner, CSV plan export

Observed. CSV export links from the planner stay valid for 7 days, and anyone with the link can open it. In the last 30 days, 3 links were opened from IP addresses outside the Netherlands. Two were Kees on holiday in Portugal. The third is unexplained, and it was not a Kestrel address. The exports contain addresses and container types, but no residents' names.

Why it matters. A forwarded link works for a week for whoever has it. We still need to find out who opened the third link.

Fix. Shorten the link lifetime to 24 hours and require a login. Ruth also checks the access log for the unexplained download and asks Kees who might have used that link.

Finding 8

Audit log is kept for 90 days, not 12 months

minorAnnex C11Tenant audit log settings

Observed. The tenant keeps its audit log for 90 days, which is the default.

Why it matters. C11 requires 12 months.

Fix. Kestrel raises retention to 12 months. It takes five minutes and costs Westerhaven nothing.
