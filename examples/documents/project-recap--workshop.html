● How it went

The shape of the delay was administrative, not technical: one credential request sat in a queue for five weeks and pushed the whole test phase. Once staging opened, the plan ran at its intended pace, and the shadow-run pattern caught one real divergence (a currency-rounding mismatch) before any customer could see it.

The riskiest call, rejecting whole orders instead of partially reserving, was made in week 1 with Atlas operations in the room. It held. The 8-second SLA turned out to be threatened by a maintenance-job lock window rather than load; the timeout analysis report covers that story and its fix.

● What we learned

  • External credentials belong on the critical path from day zero. Next engagement contracts a named credential owner and an SLA before work starts, and week 1 has a hard checkpoint on it.
  • A shadow run is cheap insurance with a long tail: keep the reconciliation job as a permanent nightly guard, not a launch-only scaffold.
  • Saying no to partial reservations early saved an estimated two weeks of invoicing edge cases. A scope cut agreed in the room beats one negotiated over email in week 7.
