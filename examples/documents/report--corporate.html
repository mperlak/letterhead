Reservation Timeout Analysis

What three weeks of staging traffic say about the 8-second reservation SLA.

Covers
May 26 – June 15, 2026 · 48,214 staged orders
Author
Integration team, Lumen Commerce
Audience
Lumen engineering, Atlas operations
Status
Final

Summary of findings

  1. Reservation timeouts cluster inside the Atlas item-master maintenance window, not at load peaks.
  2. The retry-once policy quietly recovers 92% of timeouts; customers almost never see a failure.
  3. Load alone never breached the SLA: p95 stayed at 6.1 seconds even at three times peak campaign volume.
  4. Recommended action: move the item-master maintenance job out of shopping hours; keep retry policy as designed.

What was examined and how

All reservation calls from 21 days of staging traffic (48,214 orders), including two replayed campaign patterns at 1× and 3× peak volume. Timeout events were correlated by timestamp against Atlas lock logs and the batch job schedule. Confidence is high for the lock-window finding (the correlation is stark) and medium for the load ceiling, since staging hardware is one size below production.

Finding 1: timeouts follow lock windows, not load

Finding 78% of all reservation timeouts fall inside the 02:00–02:20 window in which the Atlas item-master maintenance job holds table locks.

Order volume in that window is under 2% of the daily total, which rules load out as the driver. Regression against request volume explains only 9% of timeout variance; the maintenance window explains 71% on its own.

Implication: the SLA problem is a scheduling conflict, not a capacity problem. Hardware upgrades would not move these numbers.

Finding 2: retry-once recovers most timeouts

Finding 92% of timed-out reservations succeed on the single retry; 8% fail twice.

Of the double failures, all but three occurred inside the maintenance window. The idempotency-key design held: replayed keys returned reservation status and no double-holds were observed across the full period.

Evidence

1,247 timeout events → 1,147 recovered on retry (92.0%) → 100 failed twice, of which 97 fell in 02:00–02:20. Zero duplicate stock holds in reservation_audit.

Implication: a second retry would add checkout latency for the healthy case and rescue almost nothing; the failing 8% need the scheduling fix, not more retries.

Finding 3: load never threatened the SLA

Finding At 3× peak campaign volume, reservation p95 was 6.1s and p99 was 7.8s against the 8-second SLA.

Recommendations

  1. Reschedule the Atlas item-master maintenance job outside shopping hours, or make it lock-free for reads. Owner: Atlas IT. Follows from finding 1.
  2. Keep retry-once; do not add a second retry. Owner: Lumen engineering. Follows from finding 2.
  3. Repeat the 3× load test on production hardware before the autumn campaign window is booked. Owner: integration team. Follows from finding 3.
