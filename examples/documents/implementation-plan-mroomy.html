What we are doing, in short

Today a return means an email thread: customer writes to support, support asks the warehouse, the refund happens days later with no record connecting the three. Phase 2 gives customers a self-service returns portal, creates the return in Atlas the moment it is submitted, and refunds automatically for good-condition items. The work runs nine weeks from contract to general availability, and it starts with two decisions listed at the end of this plan.

What is in, what is out

In. Self-service returns from order history, prepaid labels, automatic refunds for grade A and B returns, a human queue for grade C, partial refunds per line item.

Out. Exchanges (modeled as return plus new order), in-store returns, refunds to store credit, cross-border returns beyond the EU.

Assumed. Phase 1 order sync is live; the warehouse grades returns within two business days.

The four steps

Step 1 · weeks 1–2

Agree the RMA event contract

Both teams sign the payload spec for rma.created and rma.received, following the pattern that worked for order sync.

Done when
  • Signed spec committed to both repos.
  • Return window rule confirmed (decision 1).
Step 2 · weeks 3–6

Build the returns portal behind a flag

Customers start a return from order history; eligible items get a prepaid label; the RMA appears in Atlas the moment the customer submits. Grade A and B returns refund automatically to the original payment method; grade C goes to a support queue with photos.

Done when
  • Internal orders flow end to end in staging: portal → RMA → receive → refund.
  • Grade C queue routes to the owner chosen in decision 2.
Step 3 · weeks 7–8

Pilot with 10% of customers

The flag opens for one customer in ten. We watch refund latency, error rate, and what people actually click.

Done when
  • Refund latency stays under 48 hours.
  • Error rate stays under 1%.
Step 4 · week 9

Open it to everyone

Flag removed, portal linked from order emails, support macros switched to point at the portal first.

Done when
  • Support ticket volume for returns drops measurably against the pre-launch baseline.

What could go wrong, and what we do then

RiskHow we noticeResponseOwner
Warehouse grading backlog delays refunds Receiving queue over 2 days Auto-refund ungraded returns after 5 business days, reconcile later Atlas ops
Self-service invites fraudulent returns Refund-to-return mismatch over 2% Grade C keeps a human in the loop; per-customer velocity limits Lumen risk
Payment provider rate-limits bulk refunds Refund API errors during campaign returns Refund queue with backoff instead of fire-and-forget calls Lumen engineering

Two decisions we need from you

#DecisionBlocks
1 Return window: keep 30 days for everyone, or extend to 60 days for loyalty-tier customers? This sets the portal's eligibility rules. Step 1 exit
2 Who owns grade C disputes: support or the warehouse team? This decides which queue tooling we build into. Step 2, from week 4
3 Back-fill RMAs for returns currently in the email pipeline? Nothing — can migrate after launch

Rollout and the way back

The pilot flag is the rollout: 10%, then everyone, reversible at any moment by closing the flag. Returns already submitted keep flowing through Atlas even if the portal closes, and the email path stays available throughout, so no customer is ever stuck mid-return.
