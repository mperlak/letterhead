## 1. Overview

Kestrel Labs documents go to people who run fleets and to the developers who integrate with the planner. They read like the product works: exact times, real figures, and nothing decorative. The base composition is the engineering style, with dense tables, mono in working roles, and explicit states.

## 2. Colors

Cobalt `#1f4fd1` is the brand colour. It is in the logo mark, the buttons, the links and the headline figures. Text is a deep navy ink, `#101828`, on white paper. Amber `#f4b73c` is the second colour. It fills tags and callouts and is never used for text.

## 3. Typography

Headings are Manrope at 700, the same face as the logo lettering. Body text is IBM Plex Sans. Code, IDs and timestamps are set in IBM Plex Mono, which the site uses for its API samples.

## 4. Elevation

The site uses flat surfaces with thin borders. Documents follow the same rule: no shadows, just hairline rules and a pale tinted panel where a block needs separating.

## 5. Components

Buttons and links are cobalt. A tag is a small amber label with ink text, as on the site's release banner. Key figures are large cobalt numbers with a short muted caption.

## 6. Do's and Don'ts

Do put the real time, number or identifier in the sentence. Don't use amber as text on paper, and don't add a second accent colour.

## 7. Composition

Not measured. The engineering style governs density, imagery and rhythm until someone supplies a screenshot or an existing Kestrel document.
