## 1. Overview

Studio Halde designs and oversees the interiors of private homes in and around Amsterdam. Its documents (proposals, briefs, site reports, styling plans) go to private clients and should read like the studio talks: calm and warm, with the practical facts stated plainly. The base composition is the atelier style.

## 2. Colors

Clay (#9a5b3f) is the one brand colour: the logo, links, key figures and rules. Linen (#ece2d4) fills callouts, tags and highlighted rows and never carries text on its own. Text is a warm near-black (#2b2522) on off-white paper (#faf7f2), the same pair the site uses.

## 3. Typography

Headings are set in Cormorant Garamond at medium weight (500), never bold, with italics for the one phrase that matters, as on the site. Body text is Jost at 400. Uppercase with wide letter-spacing is for small labels only, as in the site's navigation and buttons.

## 4. Elevation

Flat. Sections are separated by thin warm rules and linen panels, not by shadows.

## 5. Components

Buttons and tags are pill-shaped. Numbered steps use a Garamond numeral in clay ahead of each line, as the approach list on the site does. Quotes from clients are set in Garamond italic and centred.

## 6. Do's and Don'ts

Do keep one clay accent per screen and let the paper breathe. Don't set headings bold, don't put text on linen in clay at small sizes, and don't add a second accent hue.

## 7. Composition

Not measured. The atelier style governs density, imagery and rhythm until a real Halde document or a screenshot of one gives better evidence.
