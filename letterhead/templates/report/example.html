Summary of findings

  1. Six in ten reports are real misses.183 of the 300 reports were bins the truck passed and did not empty; the rest were not out, out on the wrong day or blocked.
  2. The truck's data tells them apart.GPS and lift events classified 271 reports the way the dispatcher did; 29 had no usable trace.
  3. A return stop takes six steps, five of them automatic.Only reports without a trace need a person.
  4. The hour of the report decides the return.Reports in before 13:00 were collected the same day twice as often as later ones.

What we do about it

The pilot classifies every report from the truck's data and sends only real misses back the same day.

  1. Classification from GPS and lift events from day one.
  2. Return stops only for real misses reported before the cut-off.
  3. Reports without a trace go to the dispatcher.

What we looked at and how

Kees Brouwer's dispatchers labelled each report by hand in August; we compared their labels with what the truck data says, without telling the classifier the label.

We took the 300 missed-bin reports ReinNet's dispatchers handled between 3 and 28 August 2026, with the GPS trace and the lift events of the truck that served each street that day. For every report we asked one question: did the truck pass the bin in its planned window and lift nothing?

Finding 1

Six in ten reports are real misses

The truck passed the bin and did not empty it in 183 of 300 cases. The other 117 reports would not have been helped by a return trip.

34%of real misses were collected the same day in August; the pilot's target is 80% of those reported before the cut-off.

Today a dispatcher reads every report and phones a driver. Every second call is about a bin that was never out, and nothing records whether the driver went back.

Finding 2

GPS and lift events tell them apart

Two signals the trucks already send are enough to sort a report: where the truck was, and whether its arm lifted a bin.

What the classifier reads

The 29 reports without a trace were all on four streets in the old harbour, where the trucks lose GPS between the warehouses.

The classifier never guesses. When the trace is missing it says so, and a person decides.

Finding 3

A return stop takes six steps, five of them automatic

From the report to the closed ticket, a person is needed only when the truck data cannot say what happened.

Step 3 is the only one that waits for a person, and in August it would have applied to 29 reports in four weeks.

Finding 4

The hour of the report decides the return

39% / 17%of real misses collected the same day when reported before 13:00 (55 of 142) and after it (7 of 41).

Trucks finish their routes in the early afternoon. A report that arrives after that has no truck nearby to take it.

Recommendations

Both rules use data ReinNet's fleet system already sends to the planner; nothing new is fitted to the trucks.

  1. Classify every report from the truck data. Real miss, not presented or unknown, before anyone is phoned.
    From findings 1 and 2
  2. Send trucks back only for real misses reported before the cut-off; later ones go first on tomorrow's route.
    From findings 1 and 4
  3. Keep a person for unknown reports. The dispatcher's queue takes the reports with no trace.
    From findings 2 and 3
  4. Measure the cut-off in the first month of the pilot before fixing it for the winter.
    From finding 4

The pilot starts on Monday 11 January 2027. Three questions need an answer before we configure it.

  1. Who pays for the extra kilometres of return trips, estimated at 18 to 25 km per truck-day?
  2. Do unknown reports go to the dispatcher, or does the resident get "we'll come tomorrow" automatically?
  3. Is the cut-off 13:00, or 12:00 in winter when it is dark at 17:00?

Appendix

Terms

Real miss

The truck passed within 25 m of the bin in its planned window and lifted nothing there.

Not presented

The bin was not out, out on the wrong day, or the driver tagged it "not out" on the tablet.

Lift event

The signal the truck body sends each time its arm lifts and empties a bin.

Cut-off

The latest report time for a same-day return, set per municipality.
